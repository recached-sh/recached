## 1. Answer

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is there a sustainable rate? | **Not a sharp one.** The replica fails by random events (stalls of its AOF on this HDD, §4): what the data give is a failure count that rises with the write rate (§3), counted from the load's start: **at 1,380 and 1,610 tx/s no disconnect** in any build (11 and 7 minutes per build); at 1,840 tx/s H 1 (in 39 minutes), I none (in 69), P 6 in 69 (a chain at one soak's start, then one 2.72 s stall in its rerun); at 2,070–2,300 tx/s every build several (1–6 per 7 minutes); from 3,680 tx/s 445–790 per hour. Failures come in episodes, often at the start of a link or a resynchronization, so the counts are not Poisson. (An **episode** here is one disconnect, or a chain of disconnects each within 90 s of the previous one: a failed link's successor is young too.) and the intervals in `raw/r2/stalls.md` understate the uncertainty |
| Does P lose capacity against H? | **No difference is detectable, and none is expected from the code.** At 1,380–1,840 tx/s, **unforced** failure episodes (`r2`, `r4`, `r5` before its first forced resync, `r6`, `r6b`): H 1 in 57 minutes, I 0 in 87, P 2 in 87; separately, **after a forced resynchronization** (`r5`), one more disconnect in H 2 of 8, I 1 of 8, P 2 of 8; at 2,070–2,300 tx/s: H 3, I 3, P 3 runs with failures. Counts this small cannot exclude a 3–5× difference between builds (review C N4); the stronger argument is the code: I's and P's replicas do the same I/O (#200 changed only the CPU cost of the replica's AOF record check), and P's primary differs from I's only in the backlog's group bookkeeping and its batching (#200), which change no byte on the wire. The one place P looked worse than I, the chain at the start of P's soak (not rotated, P last, no socket data), did not recur when rerun with P first (`r6`: one disconnect after a 2.72 s stall on an established link; I's rerun `r6b`: none). P's replica uses 0.38× of H's CPU per transaction, so it has more headroom, not less |
| … in frames and bytes, at 1,840 tx/s | 184,000 replicated `SET` frames/s (the primary's sent-frame counter); 14 MB/s of replica AOF before btrfs compression, 1.5–2.1 MB/s written to the disk; about 14 MB/s on the replication link |
| What saturates first | **the replica's own AOF on this HDD, through stalls, not bandwidth.** The replica's single task, which both applies frames and reads its link, blocks in an AOF `write(2)` for up to 2.7 s at a time (waiting, by inference, on the same file's `fdatasync`); for that long nothing is read from the link. When a stall outlasts the buffering between the nodes (the primary's 8 MiB queue plus the sockets, 1–2 s on this host), the primary's byte budget disconnects the replica and it full-syncs. Not CPU (at 1,840 tx/s the replica uses 0.22–0.24 CPU in I/P, 0.58–0.72 in H), not disk bandwidth (busy 13–20% of the time). Without its AOF, every build's replica keeps up with the primary's maximum (about 8,100 tx/s); with its files on tmpfs, every build sustains 2,300 tx/s, and at the primary's maximum I and P streamed about 92 s without a resync (until the host's tmpfs quota) where H, at a slightly higher accepted rate, took 21 full syncs |
| Repeated full syncs below the failure region? | **Chains, not loops.** The longest observed chain is **4 consecutive new links failing before one held** (P's soak at 1,840 tx/s, within its first 150 s; one of the four broke during its own 6.5 s install, when the 64 MiB read-ahead filled before the checkpoint finished). After 24 resynchronizations forced under load at 1,380 and 1,840 tx/s (§3.3), 5 were followed by one more disconnect within 31 s of the new link's install and the next full sync held (H 2 of 8, I 1 of 8, P 2 of 8). No run failed to settle |
| Convergence | every run with a run record matched exactly in memory after the load (the restart check, where it completed, too; it did not complete in `r4`, the tmpfs-quota runs and `r6`, and `r6` lost its record: §3.1): in `r2` and `r3`, 60 of the 66 runs **by streaming** (the other 6: I's and P's 4 tmpfs-quota runs, measured only up to the quota, and H's 2 tmpfs runs at the primary's maximum, which converged through a full sync 1.7–2.0 s after the load); in `r1`, 11 runs by streaming and 13 overloaded runs through a full sync that completed after the load (whose restart check then replays a nearly empty AOF) |
| Partial group application | not observable by these benchmarks, which check the final state: no apply or protocol error in any replica log, no resume inside a group, no partial resync at all (every disconnect in `r1`–`r5` led to a full sync); the guarantee rests on the code and 6O-I's atomicity tests |

**The rerun (`r6`, P first, then I; review D C5):** with socket sampling and the replica's autosave at the production 900 s. P's 30 minutes at 1,840 tx/s: **one** disconnect, at +638 s, on an established link (receive buffer 27.8 MB), after a 2.72 s AOF write stall, the longest seen at these rates; one full sync, then 20 minutes clean. No early chain this time: P's chain in `r4` did not recur. I's rerun (`r6b`): **no disconnect in 30 minutes**, riding through three stalls of over 1 s (up to 1.58 s), exact keyspaces. Over the five soaks at 1,840 tx/s (H `r4`; I `r4`, `r6b`; P `r4`, `r6`): H 0 failure episodes in 30 minutes, I 0 in 60, P 2 in 60 (the `r4` chain, which did not recur, and one 2.72 s stall on an established link). Counts this small do not distinguish the builds (two episodes against none), and I's and P's replicas do the same I/O. (P's `r6` run then aborted at its restart check, below.)

**A replica never autosaves, and its AOF's replay needs memory equal to the file (pre-existing, every build).** The replica's 900 s autosave never fired in `r6`: autosave requires the store's dirty count to be non-zero, and only client writes and the expiry sweeper mark it, not replicated writes (`mark_dirty` call sites, the same in H and P). A replica's AOF is therefore truncated only by its full syncs: the `r4` replicas wrote 23–25 GB of AOF in 30 minutes. AOF replay reads the whole file into memory (`tokio::fs::read` in `replay_aof_format`, unchanged since August 2026, the same in H and P): P's replica, restarted on about 11 GB of AOF in `r6`, reached 12.3 GB of RSS and was killed by the kernel's OOM killer (raw/r6/FAILED). The `r4` restart checks timed out before reaching that point. Not a 6O-I regression; a release risk carried in the gate (§5).

Disk reads were negligible (≤ 0.14 MB/s). On **established links** (after each soak's first 150 s) the five soaks hold one failure, `r6`'s at +638 s, in about 135 minutes: about 0.4 per hour, with a 95% upper bound of about 2.1 per hour pooled over the builds and 5–6.5 per hour for a single build. The `r4` runs were not rotated (one each, in sequence); `r6` ran P first.

### 3.2 When failures happen: young links and small receive buffers

Across every paced HDD run at ≤ 2,300 tx/s (`r1`–`r4`), 18 of 26 disconnects came in the first 30 s of load, 4 in the next 30 s, 4 between 60 and 300 s, and none after 300 s (76 minutes, which are only the three `r4` soaks); `r6` later had one at +638 s, on an established link with a 27.8 MB buffer after a 2.72 s stall, so late failures happen, rarely. Two candidate causes for the early ones, confounded in `r1`–`r4`:

- **The link's TCP receive buffer.** In `r5`, sampled once a second (`raw/r5/ss.txt.gz`; `rb` is the buffer's limit, not its occupancy; sampling began about 71 s into the first run, and `r1`–`r4` have none), a new replication connection started with 0.13–2.4 MB and grew by autotuning toward the 32 MiB maximum over tens of seconds, sometimes not at all: 4 of about 29 links kept ≤ 2.5 MB for at least 100 s. The five forced-resync failures came on stalls of 0.6–1.5 s with a buffer of 0.97–2.37 MB; links with 9–26 MB rode through stalls of 1.2–1.54 s. But small-buffer links also survived stalls of 0.42–0.75 s, and H's unforced failure in `r5` came on a 22.6 MB link, 105 s into the load, on a 1.75 s stall. With a small receive buffer, the buffering between the nodes is the primary's queue (8 MiB *charged*, about 0.23–0.31 s of stream at these rates), its 4 MiB send buffer and the small receive buffer; with a large one, 1–2 s or more.
- **The AOF's age.** Early in every soak the disk wrote 2.0–2.9 MB/s against 1.6 MB/s later (a fresh AOF after the replica's checkpoint), so a young link also has a young AOF.

**Status:** the receive-buffer explanation is *supported, not established*. What the data show is that **young links fail**: young links, which also have small receive buffers, young AOFs and a backlog to work through after their install. The buffer's size and the link's age cannot be separated here: every forced link started at 0.13–2.43 MB, every forced failure came 4–31 s after its install, and of the 9 links still ≤ 2.5 MB 30 s after their install, 8 survived; and since autotuning sizes the buffer from how fast the replica reads, a stalling replica keeps a small buffer (review A round 3, R3-2). A causal test needs no root but was not run: pin `SO_RCVBUF` on the replica's link, or move the replica's link reader to its own thread in a diagnostic build. Either way the mechanism belongs to the host and the replica's design (6O-H), not to 6O-I.

### 3.3 Resynchronization under load (`r5`)

At 1,380 and 1,840 tx/s, with the replica's autosave at the production default (900 s), the driver killed the replica and started it again on its own files four times during a 600 s load (at 135, 255, 375 and 495 s): each time the replica replayed its AOF (11–21 s to `PONG`, 0.6–1.5 GB), reconnected, and full-synced under load. One run per build and rate; `raw/r5/bounces.md` (`tools/bounces_i8.py`).

| forced resynchronizations followed by another disconnect before the next one | H | I | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,840 tx/s | 0 of 4 | 0 of 4 | 2 of 4 (4 and 8 s into the new link) |
| 1,380 tx/s | 2 of 4 (5 and 31 s into the new link) | 1 of 4 (10 s in) | 0 of 4 |
| **total** | **2 of 8** | **1 of 8** | **2 of 8** |

- Every resync installed (0.5–1.0 s after `PONG`); in 5 of 24 the new link failed once more within 31 s of its install (the link's age is counted from the install throughout) and the next full sync held. Every run's primary, replica and replica-restarted-from-its-own-AOF matched exactly (6 of 6), the final restart replaying the AOF accumulated since the replica's last full sync (8–16 s).
- **Limits** (review C N5): the 900 s autosave never fired within these 600 s loads (the bounces' full syncs checkpointed instead), so periodic checkpoints under load are untested; and each mid-load restart's recovered state was replaced by the full sync that followed, so only the final restart was verified. The 24 mid-load AOF replays logged no torn-tail warning.

## 4. What saturates first (`raw/r3/`: controls, 2 reps × 120 s per cell and build)

| cell | H | I | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| **no replica AOF**, 2,300 tx/s | sustained ×2 (replica CPU 101 µs/tx) | sustained ×2 (91) | sustained ×2 (91) |
| no replica AOF, 9,200 offered | the **primary** saturates (accepts 0.88 = 8,100 tx/s); the replica keeps up, no resync ×2 | the same ×2 | the same ×2 |
| **replica files on tmpfs**, 2,300 tx/s | sustained ×2 (replica CPU 196–200 µs/tx) | sustained ×2 (107–108) | sustained ×2 (104–105) |
| replica files on tmpfs, 9,200 offered (primary accepts 0.89–0.94; H's 0.94, I's and P's 0.87–0.91) | **21 full syncs** in each rep | no resync for the ~92 s its AOF could grow (6.66 GB, then the host's `/tmp` quota) | the same |
| HDD, 2,300 tx/s, diag v1 build on **both** nodes (H's replica times every frame, so its figures do not compare with I's and P's: methodology §7), measured window: longest applier stall / longest gap between two link reads | 1,202 / 1,202 ms; 1,461 / 1,460 ms | 537 / 485 ms; 1,530 / 1,468 ms | 376 / 347 ms; 640 / 547 ms |

- **The replica's AOF on the HDD is what fails first.** Without it every build's replica keeps up with the primary's maximum; with its files on tmpfs every build sustains 2,300 tx/s (on the HDD, 2,300 tx/s fails in about half the runs).
- **The mechanism is stalls, measured.** At 2,300 tx/s a single group apply (or, in H, a run of 64 frames) blocks for 0.35–1.5 s, and the replica's longest gap between two link reads matches it to within 0.1 ms: the link reader runs in the applier's task, so a blocked applier reads nothing. The stall is in the AOF `write(2)` (the write time accumulated in the stall's second equals the stall while the number of writes collapses; voluntary context switches drop, involuntary ones do not rise: the thread is blocked, not preempted). That it waits on the same file's `fdatasync` (btrfs on an HDD, mean 160–220 ms, longer tail) is inferred, not traced. Because the reader shares the applier's task, more replica workers would not help.
- **Buffering:** stalls of 1.1–1.5 s did not disconnect H or I in `r3` (H's primary queue peaked at 122 KB during its 1.46 s stall: the sockets held the rest, about 26 MB), while P's single disconnect in that cell followed a **693 ms stall during the warm-up** (the table shows the measured window only). The buffering between the nodes is therefore 1–2 s at these rates *on this host*, set by its 32 MiB `tcp_rmem` maximum; a host with the kernel's default 6 MiB would tolerate shorter stalls. A run at the default was not made (it needs root).
- **Without the disk, 6O-I's replica is the stronger one:** on tmpfs at the primary's maximum H's replica (one AOF record and `write(2)` per frame) is CPU-bound and full-syncs 21 times per run (at a higher accepted rate, 8,637 against 7,984–8,327 tx/s), while I's and P's stream without a resync for the ~92 s until the host's tmpfs quota stops their AOF. At the quota their AOF writes fail and persistence is latched unhealthy, as designed (6O-H R2-3); in rep 1 a disconnect then made every later full sync's checkpoint fail for lack of space (about 100 retries); all four of these runs then failed the restart check (a 6.66 GB AOF outlasts the driver's 60 s wait). They are reported only up to the quota.
- **btrfs `zstd`** compresses this synthetic AOF about 7×; the disk numbers are this host's and this data's (methodology §7).
