# v0.6.0 Phase 6O-G: independent review

## How the reviews were run

- **Reviewers.** Each reviewer was an agent that wrote none of the code under review. Each worked on its own detached worktree with its own Cargo target directory, wrote probe tests there, and never committed.
- **What each got.** The commit(s), the design document (told to compare against the complete revision 5, not an early revision), the brief's focus list for its review, harness pointers, and what the server does around the code. It did not get the author's correctness argument beyond what the commit messages say.
- **Probes.** Every probe that demonstrated a defect, and the stream/convergence probes that passed, were adopted as permanent tests (renamed, with their review IDs in comments).

## Review A (store transaction boundary)

Verified correct by the reviewer:
- the stripe set equals `mutation_scope` over the writes (every stripe for FLUSHDB or a capped store), and every stripe and gate acquisition in the store is handled inside the scope;
- linearizability under a load test (0 new-then-old results over every store kind with unrelated writers sharing stripes);
- the GET fast path is unchanged;
- the lock order and the absence of cycles with readers, writers, the sweeper, eviction, restore and other transactions;
- `catch_unwind` placement, restoration of the scope and the clock, poisoning recovery.

| # | Sev | Finding | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Low | On an uncapped store the repair ran after the stripes were released, which the doc denied; readers could see an empty collection | Repair in place when the other stripes can be taken with `try_lock` (deadlock-free); release-then-rebuild only when another holder has one; doc corrected. Test `a_repair_runs_under_the_held_marks_or_after_a_contended_release` |
| A2 | Medium | Mutants M8 (no capacity gate) and M9 (capped store marks a subset) survived | Tests `the_eviction_tick_and_a_capped_sweep_wait_for_a_capped_span`, `a_capped_eviction_victim_is_not_seen_gone_inside_the_span`; both mutants killed |
| A3 | Low | M5 (release before repair) survived | `transaction_fault::observe_repair` hook; the repair test asserts every stripe held and marked during the repair; killed |
| A4 | Low | Scope invariants only `debug_assert!`; eviction/sweep inside a scope would self-deadlock in release | `assert!` on the in-scope branches and on the eviction/sweep entry points (a contained panic instead) |
| A5 | Nit | Every write copied a 48-byte scope struct | One-word `TX_SCOPE_STORE` checked first |
| A6 | Nit | `repaired` set even when nothing was mutated | Documented as conservative (now `diverged`) |
| A7 | Nit | Hook armed in two steps; no stop at the last write; probed-path fault untested | Armed atomically; `readers_wait_until_after_the_last_write`; `a_panic_in_a_probed_write_is_contained_too` |

Mutation after the fixes: 11 of 11 store mutants killed (`raw/mutation/store-mutants.log`).

## Review B (two-phase EXEC)

Verified correct by the reviewer (probes that passed are now tests):
- phase 1 fully synchronous, no guard across an await, the store hold gone before phase 2;
- AOF bytes identical to per-command execution in every combination of inline/coalesced, always/no and with/without a watcher, on a capped store with evictions; replication stream equal to the AOF; exactly one batch;
- pushes per command and in order; PUBLISH order and counts;
- no reply before its AOF write (301 frames) over TCP deferred, TCP with a watcher and WebSocket;
- the effective-write rule, the OR rule, the FLUSHDB drain, the single registration fence;
- delta/QSUB convergence on nine edge cases;
- TCP/WS parity; READONLY and MISCONF unchanged.

| # | Sev | Finding | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B-1** | **High (new)** | The notification plan de-duplicated each command's keys pairwise: O(K²). With any WATCH or QSUB on the server, `MULTI; DEL k1..kN; EXEC` blocked a worker with the transaction's guards held — 3.7 s at 20k keys (debug); the loop alone 29 s at 100k keys in release, about 30 min at the 1M-argument limit | `distinct_keys`: linear (a set past 8 keys). Test `a_wide_del_in_exec_with_a_watcher_scales_linearly`: t(20k)/t(5k) = **4.1** (was 14) |
| B-2 | Low | Eviction push routes decided after the AOF deferral decision | Decided with the writes' routes, before the AOF step |
| B-3 | Low | A large EXEC holds about 3× its frames at peak (frames, the AOF batch, replication copies); the queue is capped by count, not bytes | Accepted and documented (`V0.6O-G-TWO-PHASE-EXEC.md` §memory); a byte cap is a follow-up needing a decision |
| B-4 | Nit | A read-only EXEC paid the WATCH fence; a relative-expiry write read its deadline back with nothing observing | Fence skipped for queues without writes; read-back only when frames are built in phase 1 (the fallback equals the store's deadline at the transaction instant) |
| B-5 | Low | Test gaps (inline/coalesced batch, streams, pushes, scaling, memory) | Probes B1–B6 adopted as `transaction_streams_6og.rs`; memory test added |

## Review C (failure recovery)

Verified correct by the reviewer:
- `catch_unwind` placement; nothing released or poisoned while unwinding; prefix/FAILED/NOT_RUN replies; the prefix logged, replicated and notified; durability-gated reply; the connection keeps working;
- MISCONF before phase 2; `SAVE` snapshots, truncates the AOF and clears the latch under the barrier;
- replica attach cannot fall between the panic and the reset; an old advertised id only causes an extra full sync; recursive sub-replica reset; partial resyncs do not reset (as Redis); a demoted primary's replicas full-resync correctly;
- removing run-id rotation, or the sub-replica reset, fails both recovery tests.

| # | Sev | Finding | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| C-1 | Medium | WS `EXEC` whose reply could not be sent `break`s before resynchronising clients: every browser copy stayed diverged | `resynchronise_clients` before the reply (the connection's own close still follows its reply). Test `other_sync_clients_are_resynchronised_even_if_the_exec_client_left` |
| C-2 | Medium | On TCP the resync waited for a client that does not read its large reply | Before the reply. Test `the_resync_does_not_wait_for_the_exec_clients_reader` |
| C-3 | Medium | Poison recovery incomplete: disconnect cleanup saw no claims; `SAVE` (the only way out of MISCONF) panicked on a poisoned map | Every access recovers and clears the poison. Tests `disconnect_cleanup_after_a_poisoned_ephemeral_map`, `save_after_a_poisoned_ephemeral_map` |
| C-4 | Medium | A second panic inside the repair unwound out of EXEC: persistence stayed healthy, no reset, no resync | Repair under its own `catch_unwind`; `TransactionApplied::diverged` drives fail-closed and convergence whatever the repair's outcome. Tests `a_panic_inside_the_repair_is_contained_too` (store), `a_panic_in_the_repair_still_fails_closed` (server) |
| C-5 | Low | The convergence test's "closed" check passed on a timeout | Asserts the Close code 4001; `the_executing_ws_client_gets_its_reply_then_the_sync_gap_close` |
| C-6 | Low | The failing command's own effect on ephemeral ownership is lost with the unwind | Documented limit (`fail_closed`, `V0.6O-G-PANIC-AND-RECOVERY.md`) |
| N-1 | Nit | A panicking read's reply said writes are disabled | Separate reply for reads |
| N-2 | Nit | The design's "or clear its backlog" alternative is insufficient (a caught-up replica resumes with zero frames) | The implementation rotates the run id **and** clears the backlog; noted here for the design |
| N-3 | Nit, pre-existing | An `EXEC` past the MISCONF check before another's panic still runs | Benign: its writes are logged and replicated |
| P-1 | Info, pre-existing | A panic outside a transaction's phase 1 is not contained | Follow-up |
| (B) | — | `converge` announces only the command's own keys, not keys it evicted before panicking | Covered: test `a_watch_on_a_key_the_panicking_write_evicted_is_invalidated` passes (the EXEC-time existence check catches it) |

## Review D (performance)

Two reviewers, neither of whom wrote the code or the benchmarks. Part 1 reviewed #180's capped fit check and campaign 1's methodology, and its reviewer also probed the fit check (a 20,000-seed randomized probe, a boundary probe, a contended-repair probe: adopted as `capped_fit_tests.rs`). Part 2 (a different agent; part 1's could not be resumed) reviewed campaign 2 and the profiles and recomputed every ratio from the raw logs. Their findings and resolutions are tabled in `V0.6O-G-PERFORMANCE.md` §review D. The reports are in `raw/review/review-d-part1.md` and `review-d-part2.md`.

The findings that bear on decisions:
- **D-2:** #180 deviates from design revision 5. It stays a draft until the maintainer signs off (decision D1).
- **D2-3:** #180's fit check over-counts new keys, so at the key cap it never narrows. Two options to fix that are offered with D1.
- **D2-5, D2-6:** the large-transaction reader cost is larger than the closed-loop p99 suggests, and readers park Tokio workers (decision D5).

## Comparison with the complete design

Each reviewer compared against revision 5, not an early revision. Deviations, all recorded in the implementation reports:
- full metadata rebuild instead of per-key (exactness of the size counter);
- `assert!` instead of `debug_assert!` for scope misses;
- in-place repair when possible;
- frames built in phase 1 only when observed;
- run-id rotation **and** backlog clear.

No reviewer found a part of the design left unimplemented except the separately gated framing (6O-G2), which is out of scope.
