## 1. Issue A: expiring-counter resurrection

### 1.1 Mechanism

A counter is propagated **by value**, as a `SET`, so that a replica that missed a frame converges on the primary's number instead of compounding its own. Everything downstream receives the same buffer: the AOF, the replication stream, browser and mobile sync. Each consumer applies it through `Command::from_value` and `KeyValueStore::execute`.

| Step | Code at `410235b` | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `core-engine/src/store.rs` `incr_by` (via `typed_entry!`) | `INCR` of a live key keeps the entry's `deadline` and changes the value. On an absent or **expired** entry, `typed_entry!` resets the value to `"0"` and the deadline to `NONE` first: the counter starts a new, permanent key |
| 2 | `server-native/src/propagation.rs` `broadcast_for_at`, the `Incr \| Decr \| IncrBy \| DecrBy` arm | Emits `SET <key> <n> KEEPTTL` whatever the key's deadline. User `SET … KEEPTTL` is emitted the same way |
| 3 | `KeyValueStore::set` (`SetExpiry::KeepTtl => Ok(existing_ttl)`) | Where the frame is applied, `existing_ttl` is the deadline of a **live** entry, else `None` |
| 4 | `Entry::is_expired` (`now >= deadline`) | Evaluated on the **applier's** clock, at application time |

The deadline is detached at step 2. `KEEPTTL` means "keep the deadline the key has where and when this frame is applied". The frame should describe what the primary did. A frame applied after the key's original deadline finds an expired entry (step 4). `KEEPTTL` keeps nothing (step 3), and the counter is recreated with no deadline: **permanent**.

When it happens:
- **AOF replay** after a restart that comes after the deadline. Neither snapshots nor the AOF record expirations (the sweeper removes keys without logging), so the earlier `SET … PXAT T` record replays as an expired entry and the next counter record resurrects it.
- **A replica** that applies the frame after the deadline (a lagging link, a backlog replayed by a partial resynchronisation). The same happens to a replica whose clock is ahead of the primary's, and to one whose own sweeper removed the key just before the frame arrived.
- **A transaction**: 6O-E's frozen clock lets a queued `INCR` count into a key that is live at the transaction's instant but expired by the time its frame is applied downstream. This widens the window; it is not a separate cause.

Where it does **not** happen:
- **Live execution on the primary**: `INCR` never extends or clears a live key's deadline.
- **Snapshots and full resynchronisation**: `SnapshotEntry` carries `expires_at_ms`, the snapshot leaves out entries already expired, and `restore` skips them. Checked by `a_full_resync_after_the_deadline_does_not_keep_the_counter`.
- **Replay before the deadline**: `KEEPTTL` then keeps the right deadline.

Affected commands: `INCR`, `DECR`, `INCRBY`, `DECRBY`, and a client's own `SET … KEEPTTL` (same frame, same defect). `INCRBYFLOAT` is not implemented (`cmd.rs` parses it as `Unknown`). `HINCRBY`/`HINCRBYFLOAT` propagate as `HSET`, a delta: Issue C.

### 1.2 Reproduction

First written as `counter_expiry_6of.rs` (`0dbf5d4`, tests only, on top of `410235b`), now `server-native/src/tests/expiry_replay_6of.rs` (merged in #172):
- **Time is controlled, not slept through.** Frames are written by the real TCP write path, at the real clock, with a deadline `T` one hour ahead. AOF replay (`persistence::replay_aof`) or the replica (`sync_from_primary`) then runs on a thread whose **store clock** is fixed before or after `T`, using 6O-E's `core_engine::store::at_instant` around a current-thread runtime.
- **The replica test** runs a real primary and a real replica over the replication handshake. Every frame reaches the replica after `T`.

**End to end, with the release binaries** (`counter_restart_diff.py`, `raw/f0-reproduction/redis-diff.md`). This runs the real server with `appendfsync always`, SIGKILL, and a restart after an 800 ms deadline. Redis 7.2.5 has no key. `rc-base` brings the counter back permanent.

### 1.3 Redis 7.2.5

Redis has no such window, for two reasons:
- **It propagates `INCR` as `INCR`, and every expiry as `DEL`.** That covers the sweeper and lazy expiry on access: `propagateDeletion` in `db.c`.
- **It suspends expiry where it replays.** `keyIsExpired` returns 0 while loading (`server.loading`), and on a replica, commands from the master never treat a key as expired. A replica waits for the master's `DEL`.

Replay therefore runs on the primary's original timeline. Recached logs neither expirations nor lazy resets, so it cannot use Redis's mechanism without changing what the AOF and the replication stream carry. Issue A is narrower than that: a counter frame is already a complete overwrite of the value, and only the deadline is missing from it.

### 1.4 Boundary of the fix

The first proposal changed only the counter frame, to `SET k n PXAT <deadline>`. It was implemented and then **rejected** by the independent review. A counter followed by `EXPIRE` (extend), `PERSIST` or `RENAME` replayed correctly only because `KEEPTTL` resurrected the key first. With the fix, those keys were lost, and a `RENAME` made the replay refuse the file.

The cause is the one §1.3 names: appliers evaluate expiry on their own clock, and expirations are not logged. The maintainer chose **design first** (`V0.6O-F-TTL-DESIGN.md`: logged expirations, timeless application). The resurrection stays open, and the reproductions are kept env-gated as a known baseline.

### 1.5 Restart blockers found on the way (pre-existing)

| Case | Effect | 6O-F |
|---|---|---|
| `SET … EXAT` or `EXPIREAT` with a deadline past `i64::MAX` ms | the frame's `PXAT`/`PEXPIREAT` cannot be parsed: AOF replay refuses the file, a replica breaks its link. One client command suffices | **fixed** in PR #172: refused as Redis refuses it, legacy records clamped |
| `LSET` (or `RENAME`) of a volatile key, replayed after its deadline | `ERR no such key`: AOF replay refuses the file | needs the expiry design (Issue C); reproduction gated |

## 2. Issue B: outside readers see a transaction part-way

### 2.1 Mechanism (6O-E F1, confirmed unchanged at `410235b`)

| Layer | What EXEC holds | What an ordinary reader consults |
|---|---|---|
| Server: replication-order stripes (256 `tokio::sync::Mutex`, `ReplHub::write_order`) and the unordered gate | Gate closed; the stripes of every key the queue reads, writes or watches, from before the first queued command until after the last (`lock_transaction`) | **Nothing.** GET/MGET run on the borrowed path or `execute_and_record`, straight into the store |
| Store: write-order stripes (256 × `std::sync::Mutex` + `seq` + `multi`, 6O-D) | **Each queued command separately.** `run_write_gated` takes, marks and releases its stripes inside one synchronous store call | `read_one_at` checks `multi`; `read_many` validates `seq` and falls back to the locks |

Between queued command *i* and *i+1*, `run_transaction_queue` awaits command *i*'s effects: an AOF append, under `always` with observers its `fdatasync`, replication fan-out, WATCH notification and WebSocket pushes. Through that await no store stripe is held or marked. A reader then sees command *i*'s write and not *i+1*'s. The store's protocol makes each command atomic. The server's protocol excludes writers, not readers. Nothing makes the transaction as a whole atomic to readers.

### 2.2 Reproduction

The probe from 6O-E, `an_outside_reader_never_sees_a_transaction_part_way` (`server-native/src/tests/exec_isolation.rs`), runs only with `RECACHED_PROBE_6OE_F1=1`:
- `MULTI SET a new SET b new EXEC` is stopped after `SET a` by the test-only `exec_pause` hook, with every guard held;
- another connection's `MGET a b` answers.

On `410235b` it fails 10 runs out of 10 with `[new, old]` (`raw/f0-reproduction/f1-probe-on-410235b.log`). The probe stays env-gated until the fix (CI runs ignored tests).

### 2.3 Boundary

Design only, in PR 2: `V0.6O-F-TRANSACTION-ATOMICITY-DESIGN.md`. No production change in 6O-F.

## 3. What A and B share

Nothing in mechanism. Both concern what an observer outside the primary's write sees, the downstream copy in A and a concurrent reader in B. And both are exactly the places where Recached splits one logical operation into steps that Redis performs as one. A transaction's frames are also not grouped in the AOF or the replication stream: B's design covers that (§6 of the design report).

## 4. Issue C (new): delta frames replayed after the deadline

A frame that changes *part* of a value has the same exposure as a counter, with no cure available inside the frame itself. Examples: `APPEND`; `HSET` (also what `HINCRBY`/`HINCRBYFLOAT` propagate); `RPUSH`/`LPUSH`; `SADD`; `ZADD`; `SETRANGE`.

`SET s a`, `PEXPIREAT s T`, `APPEND s b` (while live), replayed after `T`:
1. The `SET` creates `s`, and the `PEXPIREAT` expires it.
2. The `APPEND` then finds an expired entry. `typed_entry!` resets it and clears its deadline, and the result is `s = "b"`, permanent.

The reproduction `reproduce_partial_updates_replayed_after_the_deadline` (`RECACHED_REPRODUCE_6OF_COUNTER=1`) resurrects all three of `APPEND`, `HINCRBY` and `RPUSH` on `410235b` and on `dev` after 6O-F.

There is no frame-local cure. A counter frame is a complete overwrite, so it can carry the deadline. A delta frame cannot express "the key's deadline is still `T`" without a second command. A correct general fix needs one of:
1. **Redis's model.** Log an explicit `DEL` whenever the primary removes an expired key: sweeper removals and lazy resets by writes. Replay and replica application then never evaluate expiry. This changes what the AOF and the replication stream carry: two or more records per reset, and a replica that no longer expires on its own. It is not compatible with AOF files written before the change: their records assume the replayer expires.
2. **Self-describing deltas.** Follow every delta to a key with a deadline by `PEXPIREAT k T`, and precede a write that reset an expired key with `DEL k`. No new command, but more records per write and a frame-count change for replication offsets.

Either one is a persistence and replication format decision. Option 1 is the same mechanism the counter design needs: `V0.6O-F-TTL-DESIGN.md` Option E covers Issues A and C together.

- **Severity:** Medium, pre-existing. It is the same class as A, but it needs the expiry, the delta and the late application to coincide. Collections with TTLs (session hashes, rate windows as lists) are exposed.
- **Recommended:** a maintainer decision on option 1 or 2 as its own milestone, before or after v0.6.0. Recorded in the release gate.
