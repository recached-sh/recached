## PR 0a: DEDUP acknowledgement follows the AOF stage (#128)

**Finding.**
- On the deferred TCP path, a DEDUP write committed its mark once its frame was queued. A replay on another connection, whose gate holds no ticket for that batch, was answered `+DUP` before the original's `write(2)`, or its `fdatasync` under `always`.
- A failure of that batch then lost a write a client had been told was applied.
- The WS early-replay path reads marks without `dedup_order`.
- A permanent refusal was remembered before its frame was appended.

**Implementation.**
- `on_write_logged`: `may_defer && dedup.is_none() && !replicas.is_enabled()`. DEDUP writes and outcomes never defer.
- `record_dedup_refusal` logs, then remembers.
- The test-only `AofHold` hook holds the batch performer on a marked batch, in process or via `RECACHED_TEST_AOF_HOLD` in the fail-stop child.

**Tests.** `tests/dedup_ack_order.rs`:
- Under `no`, `everysec` and `always`, with the original's batch held: no `+DUP`, no readable mark, the original took the inline path, and after release the result is one application with the frame in the file.
- The refusal is not remembered until its frame is written.
- Unrelated DEDUP clients are fine, and plain writes still defer.

`aof_fail_stop::a_dedup_replay_during_a_failing_always_batch_is_never_answered` (subprocess, `fsync:eio` and `fail:eio`): the replay is never answered, and the retry after restart applies exactly once.

**Mutations.**

| Mutation | Tests that fail |
|---|---|
| Revert the condition | The in-process test and the subprocess test |
| Remember before log | The refusal test |

**Review.** APPROVE WITH NITS.
- Every primary-side path that exposes an outcome is now ordered. Fail-stop and the non-fatal latch are preserved, and there is no lock inversion.
- Nits addressed: the global `dedup_order` serialization cost is stated in the PR; the hold count resets per arming; the worker-blocking caveat is documented.

**CI.** All 13 checks green.

## PR 0c: QSUB initial state follows the AOF stage (#129)

**Finding.** A TCP write with no observer defers its AOF wait. A QSUB on a WS socket not routed for that key took the barrier and sent `qstate` at once. Under `always`, a browser could be shown a value that never became durable.

**Implementation.** Cut, then wait:
1. Under the all-write barrier, record `AofWriter::tail()`, the completion of the newest batch ever queued (`AofQueue::newest`).
2. Release the stripes.
3. `AofTail::settled()` leads, then waits for that batch.
4. Send `qstate`.

If the writer stopped (`always`), the socket is closed without `qstate`. There is no wait with the AOF disabled or nothing queued.

**Tests.** `tests/qsub_aof_cut.rs`:
- `qstate` waits for a held deferred write under all three policies, then shows it.
- Three QSUBs waiting, with QUNSUB/QSUB churn, leave the all-write barrier free.
- An `always` fsync failure closes the socket (asserted explicitly) with no `qstate`, and withholds the write's reply.
- Nothing pending means no wait.

**Mutations.**

| Mutation | Tests that fail |
|---|---|
| No wait | 3 |
| Send after an `always` failure | 1 |

**Review.** APPROVE WITH NITS.
- Confirmed: in-order performance makes the tail sound; deferral always happens under stripes; no lock is held across the await; cancellation is safe; `qstate` is still sent first.
- Nits addressed: an explicit close assertion, a doc note on the tail's effect on batch statistics, and corrected test comments.

**CI.** All 13 checks green.

## PR 0d: replica-side DEDUP ordering and promotion safety (#130)

**Finding.** On a chained replica:
- Mark-only and refusal records were applied with no stripe and no `still_following` check. They could fall between a sub-replica's cut and its registration, and were applied after `REPLICAOF NO ONE`.
- An envelope committed its mark before its append (RV-14).
- **Found in review:** a full resync installed the DEDUP table outside the data's barrier.

**Implementation.**
- Mark-only and refusal records take `lock_commands_and_keys(&[], once(client))`. Verified to take the client id's real stripe; `lock_commands(&[])` takes none (NEW-3).
- `still_following` is checked under that stripe.
- All three replica paths log before committing or remembering.
- `replace_dedup_table` moved inside the full-resync barrier.

**Tests.** `replication::dedup_order_tests`:
- A record arriving after the cut read the table reaches the sub-replica through the stream.
- A record pending across a promotion, or arriving after it, is refused and leaves no trace.
- A record waits only for its client's stripe.
- The existing `replication_dedup` and `replica_6l` suites (full and partial resync, promotion) pass.

**Mutations.**

| Mutation | Tests that fail |
|---|---|
| No stripe | 3 |
| No `still_following` | 2 |

**Review.** APPROVE WITH NITS.
- F1, the table install, was fixed.
- N1, the race test, was rewritten so the dangerous interleaving fails outright.
- N2, the promotion test bypassing `promote_to_primary`, was accepted.

**CI.** All 13 checks green.

## PR 0b: refused WS commands take no global barrier (#132)

**Finding.**
- `DEDUP c n FLUSHDB` on a strict socket is refused as administrative. The refusal took the command's stripes: every stripe with the gate closed, through an append.
- **Review round 1, High:** a refused many-key `DEL` took one stripe per key, which amounts to the same barrier.

**Implementation, and a deviation from 6N-B.**
- 6N-B proposed a lock shared with SAVE. That does not exclude `handle_replica`'s cut, and the refusal is also a replication frame. The reviewer confirmed the gap.
- Instead, both WS refusal branches use `record_unexecuted_dedup_refusal`: `dedup_order`, then the DEDUP identity's single stripe (`ReplHub::lock_identity`). That one stripe excludes every all-stripe barrier (SAVE, replica cut, promotion, QSUB), and it leaves the gate open.
- Per-connection pacing at 32 recorded refusals/s, with a token re-check after pacing.
- The hook hands a held worker off with `block_in_place`, and only for a marked batch.

**Tests.** `tests/ws_refusal_barrier.rs`:
- Refused FLUSHDB and refused 2,001-key DEL, held mid-append: a writer on another stripe proceeds.
- A refusal inside a checkpoint window is neither remembered nor appended.
- A refusal sent after a replica cut read the table reaches the replica through the stream.
- A SAVE during a refusal keeps it in the sidecar across a restart.
- A flood of refusals is paced, and other writers stay free.
- Another tenant is unaffected.

**Mutations.**

| Mutation | Tests that fail |
|---|---|
| The old all-stripe lock | the FLUSHDB stall test |
| The old keyed lock | the DEL stall test |
| No identity stripe | the window test and the cut test |
| No pacing | the flood test |

**Review.**
- Round 1: CHANGES REQUIRED (the High above).
- Round 2: APPROVE WITH NITS.

**CI.** All 13 checks green on the rebased head `146258c`.

## PR 0e: unauthenticated WS sockets do not disable the fast path (#131)

**Finding.** `subscribe_pushes` counted every WS socket at connect, before AUTH. One anonymous socket forced every writer onto the striped path.

**Implementation.**
- A subscription routed `Nothing` is not counted and closes no gate.
- `ReplHub::route_pushes`:
  - **first routed:** close the gate, publish the routes, then count;
  - **routed → routed:** no re-count;
  - **routed → nothing:** uncount.
- Drop uncounts.

**Limit.** An open server with no password authenticates sockets at connect, so they are still counted there, consistent with their access.

**Tests.**

`tests/ws_fastpath.rs`:
- Unauthenticated and failed-AUTH sockets leave the fast path on.
- AUTH counts the socket; disconnect releases it.
- Strict: counted only from the token, renewal and narrowing do not double count, and revocation releases.
- Token-auth mode: counted only from the token, and expiry releases.
- 40 concurrent sockets leave no count behind.

`write_gate::websocket_auth_waits_for_an_unordered_write`.

**Mutations.**

| Mutation | Tests that fail |
|---|---|
| Counting before AUTH | 2 |
| Routing without the gate | 1 |

**Review.** APPROVE WITH NITS. Nits addressed: the token-auth test, the router doc, and the open-mode limit stated.

**CI.** All 13 checks green on `1632e10`. That is the exact tree that became `dev` `d0b9043`, which was rebased onto #132. Locally on that tree: 1294 passed, 0 failed.

## Follow-up: findings of the integration review (#133)

**Findings** (independent review of the five fixes combined, at `d0b9043`):
- **M2:** QSUB's tail wait from 0c runs inside the command handler, so the token-expiry timer arm is not polled during it. A disk stall could outlast the token, and `qstate` was then sent under a lapsed token.
- **L1:** a scoped WS client's refused DEDUP write on a *replica* was recorded as a permanent refusal locally. It went into the replica's own AOF and table and was fanned out to its sub-replicas, with no `still_following` check.

**Implementation.**
- The token is re-checked after the tail wait, and the socket is closed with `WS_CLOSE_TOKEN_EXPIRED`.
- `record_unexecuted_dedup_refusal` records nothing on a replica. The check runs under the identity stripe, which promotion's barrier excludes, so it cannot race a promotion.
- The client is still answered `NOSCOPE`, deliberately unchanged, because changing it would be protocol-visible. The out-of-order-replay caveat is documented in the code.

**Tests** (`tests/ws_refusal_barrier.rs`):
- A QSUB whose wait outlasts its token is closed, with no `qstate`.
- A refused write on a replica leaves no table entry, AOF frame or replication frame.
- Each test fails without its fix.

**Review.** APPROVE WITH NITS. L1 was confirmed race-free, and M2's teardown confirmed clean.

**CI.** All 13 checks green. Locally: 1296 passed.
