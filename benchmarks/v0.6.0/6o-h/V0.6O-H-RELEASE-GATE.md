## 6. Engineering assessment (brief §18)

1. **Is counter resurrection fixed?** Yes, through restarts (`no`/`everysec`/`always`, snapshot + AOF, multiple restarts) and on capable replicas. The proof is the 6O-F reproductions, which now pass unconditionally, and the Redis differential.
2. **Are partial-update resurrection cases fixed?** Yes: `APPEND`, `HINCRBY`, `RPUSH`/`LPUSH`, and `SET … KEEPTTL`, written while live and replayed after the deadline.
3. **Does `LSET`/`RENAME` replay now succeed?** Yes. A format file replays without evaluating expiry, so the volatile list or source key is present when the record applies. The restart diff matches Redis.
4. **Why does the design preserve the original timeline?** The primary records every expiry transition it acts on as a `DEL`, at the point in its order where it acted. Replay and capable replicas apply records without consulting a clock, so each record meets the state the primary had when it executed it. Deadlines stay in the records (`PEXPIREAT`), so reads still mask expired keys, but no later clock decides a removal.
5. **How are expired keys captured?** Each logged write runs in an observing scope (`observing_expirations`). Before the write, the store removes the expired entries among the command's `mutation_scope` keys, under its stripes, and reports each one. The sweeper reports through `sweep_expired_reporting`. A thread-local collects the keys; the clock is frozen for the scope.
6. **Can a write fail while still requiring an expiry `DEL`?** Yes. The removal already happened in memory, so the `DEL` is logged alone, without a frame. Its WebSocket push carries origin 0, so the writer receives it too (B-1). Tests: `a_write_that_fails_or_changes_nothing_still_logs_what_it_removed`, `in_exec_only_a_failed_writes_del_reaches_the_writer`.
7. **How are active sweeper deletions propagated?** Under the all-write barrier the sweeper already held, as `DEL k1 k2 …` frames of at most 64 KiB of key bytes. They go to the AOF and replicas in order, and to sync clients one key per push, filtered by tenant. A capable replica runs no sweeper.
8. **Are the `DEL`s ordered correctly?** Yes:
   - a write's observed `DEL`, its frame, then its eviction `DEL`s, in one AOF batch;
   - on replication, the `DEL` frame first;
   - inside EXEC, per command;
   - with DEDUP, before the envelope.

   Review B tried to construct violations; the one it found (B-1, push origin) is fixed. Mutants on each ordering are killed.
9. **Which AOF marker was selected?** A `RECACHED-CHECKPOINT` control record whose id is `0xEC00<<48 | kind<<40 | layout<<32 | payload`: `FORMAT` v1 with feature bit 0 (`expirylog`), id `17005593296757587969`, at offset 0. Authorship comes with it: in a format file, records are RESP2 arrays, while every older server writes pushes.
10. **What evidence proves backward compatibility?**
    - `tools/marker_compat.py`: 60/60 checks on real v0.4.0, v0.5.1, v0.5.2, `410235b` and `f9a364d` binaries;
    - CI's compat-matrix legs (v0.4.0, v0.5.1), including a crash-downgrade-upgrade;
    - review C's fake-primary runs against old replicas.
11. **Can older binaries open the new AOF?** Yes. They take `FORMAT` for a checkpoint record they skip, and replay the arrays. Files holding `COPY` (or DEDUP records, for 0.4) still stop an older binary, as before 6O-H.
12. **What limitations remain when downgrading?** The older binary evaluates expiry by its own clock, so the resurrection class applies while it runs. Its appended pushes end the timeless part, so a re-upgrade replays them by legacy rules. The procedure is unchanged: `SAVE`, stop, start the older binary.
13. **Can a legacy AOF be upgraded without rewriting historical state incorrectly?** Yes. A legacy file replays by legacy rules and stays legacy, with no rewrite, until its next checkpoint. That checkpoint writes a snapshot of the state as replayed, then `FORMAT`. History already resolved under the old rules is kept as it was; it cannot be recomputed.
14. **Does timeless replay work after checkpoint truncation?** Yes. `FORMAT` is queued in the truncation's own batch. A format snapshot keeps every entry, and the snapshot is paired with how its records replay. Tests: `restarts_and_checkpoints_keep_the_primarys_timeline` and `the_snapshot_pairs_with_how_its_records_replay`; the restart diff's `snapshot-then-aof` and `multiple-restarts`.
15. **Does the replica negotiate `caps=expirylog` correctly?** Yes:
    - it accepts it only when offered;
    - it records the mode in its resume point;
    - a mode change forces a full sync;
    - it relays the capability down a chain only when its own keyspace came from a capable primary.
16. **What happens when the primary is older?** The capability is not offered. The new replica behaves as before 6O-H: expiry evaluated on apply, its own silent sweeper, a legacy AOF (`leave_expiry_log`).
17. **What happens when the replica is older?** A 0.6 replica (6O-F/6O-G) ignores `expirylog1`, applies the extra `DEL`s harmlessly, and keeps its old clock-based behaviour. A 0.5 replica is refused while DEDUP replication is on, as before.
18. **Can delayed replicas resurrect an expired key?** A capable replica cannot: it applies frames timelessly and removes keys only on the primary's `DEL`. A replica paused past the deadlines, then promoted and restarted, matches Redis. An old-version replica keeps its old behaviour.
19. **Does promotion correctly re-enable primary expiration behaviour?** Yes:
    - `writes_expiry_log` becomes true;
    - the sweeper runs and logs;
    - writes log what they find expired;
    - the node advertises `expirylog1`;
    - a replica whose AOF was in the format leaves it at startup (R3-1), so a silent sweep can never precede timeless records.
20. **Are replication offsets and backlog handling correct?** Yes. The `DEL`s are ordinary frames with their own offsets. Partial resync is refused across a mode change. Two handshake races were found and fixed: a sweep queued behind a full sync (D1), and a stale advertisement (D2). A full sync checkpoints the replica's files before any further frame is acknowledged. #190 keeps the link drained during the checkpoint and the catch-up that follows, so a backlog no longer overflows the primary's queue into a loop of full syncs.
21. **Is Phase 6O-G atomicity preserved?** Yes. The purge happens inside each command's step of the two-phase EXEC, under the transaction's stripes, and its `DEL`s are in the transaction's one batch. No await is held under a synchronous lock. Crash and replica atomicity remain 6O-I's scope.
22. **Are DEDUP and client synchronization preserved?** Yes:
    - **DEDUP:** the `DEL` is outside the envelope, and identities are unchanged.
    - **WebSocket:** the `DEL` comes before the write, with the writer's origin when its frame follows.
    - **Tenants and WATCH:** per-key pushes respect tenant scopes, and `WATCH` sees the removal.
    - **Delta clients:** they converge (6O-G test).
23. **What is the AOF and replication traffic increase?**
    - Workloads without expiring keys: none (1.000 records/op, same bytes).
    - Counters that expire mid-run: +1.3% bytes.
    - `SET PX 1000`: +11% bytes.
    - `SET PX 100`: +30% AOF bytes and +45% replication stream bytes/op.
    - Sweeper: 23.1 bytes per swept key.
24. **What are the CPU, RSS and p99 changes?**
    - **CPU:** +1.6–3.7% per logged write, and +16–30% on `PX` workloads, tracking the added records. None without persistence or replicas.
    - **p99:** +0.008 ms on ordinary logged writes, +0.06–0.12 ms on `PX`.
    - **Primary RSS:** unchanged.
    - **Capable replica:** holds the primary's key count, where a base replica held tens of thousands fewer keys because it swept them early by its own clock. Across a 10 s stall and the resync that follows, its peak RSS was 74–82 MB on `final`, against base's 61–68 MB. With #190 it is 94–95 MB, because the backlog of the catch-up now waits on the replica, bounded at 128 MiB per link, rather than overflowing the primary's queue. During the stall the primary's peak RSS was also 8–22 MB higher; that is not attributed (performance §5.4).
25. **What still blocks v0.6.0?**
    - this phase's conditions (§3);
    - the scorecard gates not met (§7);
    - the 6O-G decisions D1–D5;
    - a release-candidate soak;
    - 6O-I, if crash and replica transaction atomicity are wanted for v0.6.0.
