# v0.6.0 Phase 6N-C5C: multi-worker coalescing and p99

**Change:** PR 3 (`perf/v0.6n-c5-multiworker-coalescing`). It implements review F1 and nothing else.

**Answer:**
- F1 (no yield while another performer is writing) raises 4-worker throughput by 8% and cuts back-offs by 74%.
- F2 (observe once per batch) was measured and **not adopted**.
- Neither F1 nor F2 is the cause of the 4-worker p99 regression. The cause is **worker oversubscription in the benchmark layout**: 4 tokio workers on 2 CPUs. With one worker per CPU, `coalesced` has *better* p99 than `inline`.

### Reading

- **F1** works as designed. One fan-out append in five found a performer busy (0.205/cmd). Skipping those yields:
  - cut futile yields;
  - cut back-offs from 17.9k to 4.6k per run, because busy-time yields had been credited as misses;
  - cut AOF `write(2)` per command from 0.43 to 0.34;
  - raised throughput 8% and lowered CPU 6%.

  p99 did not move.
- **F2** made each batch's opener the only observer. The opener is the first writer to resume, before writers on other workers join, so it sees more one-frame batches: yields per command fell to 0.105, and back-offs stayed high. With F1, F2 cut yields so far that batching grew (4.7 frames per batch), but p99 rose to 2.25 ms. That range (2.215–2.271) lies outside every other cell's.

  The brief says to prefer p99 stability, so **F2 is not adopted**. "Observe once per batch" may still be worth revisiting with a different observer, such as the performer. It is not needed now: with F1, the back-off is no longer distorted by busy-time yields.
- **F4** (the connection gate) was not touched. Profiling gave no standalone evidence for it: `rc-standalone` lead-busy and contention counters are zero at one worker.

## What the p99 regression is

All coalesced variants sit at **about 2.0 ms p99**, with tight ranges, whatever the yield policy. A diagnostic run, 5 repetitions each, isolated it:

| Cell | Workers on CPUs | AOF sync | inline p99 | coalesced p99 | coalesced ops/s vs inline |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 6N-C4 / ablation layout | 4 on 2 | everysec | 1.02 | 2.04–2.06 | 1.19× |
| **no fsync** | 4 on 2 | **no** | 0.911 | **2.071** | 1.24× |
| **no oversubscription** | **2 on 2** | everysec | 0.727 | **0.647** | 1.24× |

| Hypothesis | Verdict |
|---|---|
| The everysec `fdatasync` stalls batches | **Rejected.** With `appendfsync no` the regression remains: 2.07 against 0.91 ms. |
| F1 (busy-leader yields) | **Rejected.** F1 removes them and p99 does not change. |
| F2 (unstable back-off) | **Rejected.** The back-off varies by 20× across variants and p99 does not change. |
| **Oversubscription: more tokio workers than CPUs** | **Supported.** With 2 workers on 2 CPUs, coalesced p99 is 11% *better* than inline and throughput is +24%. Mechanism: a coalesced writer's `yield_now` is deferred on its own worker and resumes only when that worker parks or reaches its maintenance tick. When the OS deschedules that worker thread to run another worker on the same CPU, the deferred writer waits out a scheduler timeslice, in the millisecond range. Inline writers never defer, so they are exposed only through the AOF hand-off. |

**Confidence: high for the factor isolated** (one variable changed per cell, 5 repetitions, ranges that do not overlap). The kernel-level mechanism, CFS timeslices on descheduled worker threads, is inferred. Confirming it needs `sched` tracing, which `perf_event_paranoid=2` does not allow.

### Remaining scheduling limitations

- **Coalesced mode is sensitive to oversubscription.** Deployment guidance: run `RECACHED_WORKER_THREADS` ≤ the CPUs available to the process. The 6N-C4 "4-worker p99 +79%" result measures an oversubscribed layout.
- A held yield is bounded by the worker's next park or maintenance tick (61 polls). Behind a long synchronous poll on that worker, a coalesced writer waits that long. This is the same bound as the replica sender's held flush in [PR 2](V0.6N-C5-REPLICATION-OPTIMIZATION.md).
- The final 4-worker numbers, F1 included, are in [performance](V0.6N-C5-PERFORMANCE.md#four-workers).
