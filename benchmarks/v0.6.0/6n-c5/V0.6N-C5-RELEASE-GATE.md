## The ten questions

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How much CPU does replication fan-out cost? | **1.38 µs/op at P=1 and 1.33 at P=16** (`coalesced` with a replica minus without). Of that, **0.92 / 1.08 µs is system time**. Measured over 3 repetitions with a user/system split; user instructions +1,154/op. |
| 2 | Which component dominates? | **Replica sender flushes.** Each is a socket send on loopback (TCP plus the replica's receive path) and an acknowledgement read back, about 3.3–4.1 µs of kernel time each. On top of that, the sender's own wake-and-flush work is about 950 user cycles/op. The sender flushed every 3.6–3.8 frames, because tokio wakes it into the LIFO slot right after one writer. The per-frame suspects are small: frame copy ≤ 81 cycles/op, backlog about 350, `try_send` about 230, stream lock uncontended. |
| 3 | What was implemented? | **#141:** the sender still drains on every wake, but holds the socket send until the end of the scheduling round (`RoundEnd`) or a full 512-frame flush. Channels under 2048 frames flush on every wake. **#142:** a coalesced writer does not yield while another performer is writing (review F1). **#140:** INFO counters for sender flushes, frames, queue peak and held flushes. |
| 4 | How much did it deliver? | `coalesced` P=1: **−0.68 µs/op, +13.2%, p99 −12%**. P=16: **−0.82 µs/op, +34.6%, p99 −29%**. That removes about half and about 60% of the replica increment, and about 3/4 of its kernel cost. Every replicated mode gains: inline +7% at P=1, replication without AOF +11% at P=1. Four workers: +12.5% (F1 cut back-offs 4×). Replica lag rises by up to about 1 ms (p50) and 2 ms (p99). |
| 5 | Did P=1 reach 0.85× standalone? | **Yes: 0.921**, and ≥ 0.868 against every standalone reference, including 6N-C4's. |
| 6 | Did four-worker p99 improve? | **Not in the 6N-C4 layout** (4 workers on 2 CPUs): 2.18 against 1.98 ms, with run-to-run movement of about ±0.2 ms. The cause is **oversubscription**. It is not F1/F2 or the fsync: each was isolated. With 2 workers on 2 CPUs, coalesced p99 is **0.551 ms**, against the baseline's 0.703 and inline's 0.695. |
| 7 | Is P=16 still the per-command AOF wait? | **Yes.** Replication without AOF reaches 1.02× standalone at P=16, while the replicated coalesced path reaches 0.61×. The sender now batches 30 frames per flush there, as many as without AOF, so the remaining gap is the AOF stage each command waits for, one at a time, before its fan-out. |
| 8 | Does the remaining gap justify Option A? | It is the **only identified design** that targets the P=16 gap. See the assessment below. It needs maintainer approval, and Option A does not address the release blockers. |
| 9 | Are all correctness and security guarantees preserved? | **Yes**, with one recorded, **pre-existing** exposure (review R1): a scheduling round of deep pipelines larger than the replica channel can overrun a queued sender and force a resync. This also happened before 6N-C5 (8 of 10 runs, against 10 of 10 now, in the adversarial reproduction). Queue peaks are the same with either sender. |
| 10 | What remains before v0.6.0? | See the blockers below. Throughput against Redis (AOF P=1, no persistence), memory per key, and one release-candidate soak. 6N-C5 resolves none of them. |

## Option A: assessment for the maintainer

| | Assessment |
|---|---|
| What it would remove | The **per-command AOF wait before fan-out**. Effects would be staged in the batch and released by the performer after the batch's write. This is 6N-C4's bottleneck #2, now the only large P=16 cost left on the replicated path. |
| Measured ceiling | Replication without AOF: **482k at P=16 (1.02× standalone), 1.86 µs/op**. Final `coalesced`: 288k, 2.89 µs/op. With no replica, AOF `coalesced` was 328k against standalone's 465k (profile), so the wait costs about 30% even before replication. |
| Projected benefit (estimate, not measured) | Assume the per-command wait goes, and the AOF `write(2)` (0.3–0.5 µs/op at P=16, measured) plus the ordered path stay. That projects about **2.2–2.4 µs/op** at P=16, or roughly **0.75–0.95× standalone**. The range is wide because part of the wait is latency (scheduling rounds in the closed loop), not CPU. **Option A would not change P=1**, which already passes M1. |
| Costs and risks | The ordering and synchronization machinery of 6N-B: staged effects, performer-released fan-out, watermarks. 6N-B's review conditions (R3-1 and the rest) still apply. It also interacts with the sender change: a performer releasing a batch's effects would wake the sender once per batch, which suits #141's held flush. |
| Recommendation | **Option A is the right candidate if P=16 M1 is required for v0.6.0.** It is not needed for P=1. It should not displace the release blockers below, which it does not touch. **Explicit maintainer approval is required before any implementation.** |
