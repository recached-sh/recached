# v0.6.0 Phase 6O-B: independent review

Each production PR was reviewed by an independent agent that did not write it. Each reviewer had the diff and the brief's invariants, not the author's conclusions, and was asked to construct counterexamples. The reviews were read-only: no builds ran while the native benchmark was on the host. Each reviewer's report is summarized below, finding by finding.

## PR 2 (`perf/v0.6o-b-request-path`, reviewed at `c82ee97`)

| # | Severity | Finding | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Medium**, a regression in the PR | The overflow arm was armed from subscription state at the top of the loop. A `SUBSCRIBE … UNSUBSCRIBE` or `WATCH … UNWATCH` within **one read batch** leaves no state behind. An overflow signalled in between (a PUBLISH over 8 MiB, or the connection's own write over 8 MiB to a key it watches) then never disconnected the connection. Worse, it fired later at an unrelated WATCH or SUBSCRIBE. Before the PR, the next `select!` saw it | **Fixed.** `overflow_armed = true` is set at the three places a sender is handed to a registry: SUBSCRIBE, PSUBSCRIBE, WATCH. The derived update was removed. New test: `an_overflow_signalled_and_unwatched_within_one_batch_still_disconnects`. Another connection first stores a 9 MiB value at `k`; the watcher then pipelines `WATCH k`, `APPEND k x`, `UNWATCH` in one small write. It **fails on `c82ee97`** (no disconnect in 10 s) and passes on `dev`'s `connection.rs` and on the fix. A first version that sent the 9 MiB `SET` itself did not discriminate: that payload spans hundreds of reads, and each read goes back through the top of the loop. Also added: `a_pattern_subscriber_whose_queue_overflows_is_disconnected` |
| 2 | **High**, pre-existing on `dev`, not caused by the PR | When the first change to a watched key exceeds the 8 MiB notification budget, `notify` signals overflow and the registration is dropped, but nothing reaches `watch_rx`. An `EXEC` in the same batch then succeeds although the key changed. Across batches it succeeds whenever `select!` (unbiased) polls the read arm first | **Not fixed in 6O-B** (out of scope: WATCH semantics). Recorded as a release blocker candidate in the [release gate](V0.6O-B-RELEASE-GATE.md), with the reviewer's fix: mark the transaction dirty on a watch overflow, or have EXEC check `overflow_rx`. The PR neither widens nor narrows the window |
| 3 | Low | The docs said "a reply waits at most for commands that were already runnable". Tokio wakes a yielded task only after its whole pass: the local queue (including tasks that new input made ready), stolen work and a driver poll, or at the 61-poll maintenance tick | **Fixed** in `reply_coalesce.rs`, `docs/server/configuration.md` and the CHANGELOG, aligned with the AOF gate's own description |
| 4 | Low | Test gaps: (a) the WATCH arming term was untested; (b) the one-batch case; (c) the lone-connection test did not check that back-off engages; (d) no coalescing test with AOF on; (e) env parsing | (a), (b): new tests above. (c): a test-only per-thread back-off counter, now asserted. (d): the `aof_pending` condition only decides which of two yields applies. Correctness is the gate's, covered by the gate suites. Documented as performance-only. (e): the parser is a copy of `RECACHED_AOF_COALESCE`'s; not tested separately |
| 5 | Nit | The comment said a flush with AOF tickets "is yielded by the gate instead". The gate does not yield when its coalescer is backed off or off | **Fixed** (comment reworded). Performance-only |
| 6 | Nit | Resuming on another worker counts as a gain, so under heavy stealing back-off may never engage | **Accepted**, documented in `end()`. **Measured after merge**: with 3 workers, one client costs +3% CPU per op against the same binary with `RECACHED_REPLY_COALESCE=off` (every repetition), with no throughput effect; one worker is unaffected. Consistent with this finding. Recorded as a follow-up in the [release gate](V0.6O-B-RELEASE-GATE.md) ([performance](V0.6O-B-PERFORMANCE.md#multiple-workers-v2-and-the-v3-control)) |
| 7 | Nit | A non-UTF-8 `RECACHED_REPLY_COALESCE` value is treated as unset (on) | **Accepted**: identical to `RECACHED_AOF_COALESCE` |

Checked and confirmed correct by the reviewer:
- **The yield happens in the `select!` handler.** No other arm is polled and no lock or guard is held.
- **It cannot delay an unled AOF batch.** It is skipped when tickets are queued, only this task pushes them, and `flush` still goes through `poll_settled`.
- **Unchanged:** fail-stop under `always`, DEDUP ack order, reply bytes and order.
- **Cancellation during the yield** strands nothing.
- **`queued`** is updated under the lock, and every `Arc<DeferredAof>` holder is on the connection's task.
- **The thread-local state** cannot panic at teardown.
- **`select!`** always has an enabled arm.
- **Unaffected:** the WebSocket handler and replication.
- **Fairness and buffering** are bounded as before.

## PR 3 (`perf/v0.6o-b-command-collections`, reviewed at `cc53de3`)

| # | Severity | Finding | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Low | The by-reference arms (`&[u8]` pushes, `SetMember for &str`) were checked only for accounting, on a fresh store per step. A wrong SADD count by reference would be caught only by `redis_diff` (CI) | **Fixed.** `probed_writes_account_exactly_like_the_general_path` now runs the whole script on one persistent by-reference store and compares every reply, plus memory and exact accounting, with the probed store at every step |
| 2 | Nit | Dropping LPUSH alone from `probe_eligible` would fail no test | **Fixed.** New `lpush_rpush_and_sadd_each_take_the_reserved_path` asserts that each command takes the reservation fast path, including a WRONGTYPE that releases its reservation. LPUSH was also added to the eight-thread capped race. A new mutant covers it |
| 3 | Nit | Unused `AsRef<str>` supertrait on `SetMember` | **Fixed** |
| 4 | Low, pre-existing | `from_value`'s `filter_map(take_bytes)` turns a frame with a non-bulk element into a zero-value LPUSH (`:0`) where Redis refuses it | **Not changed.** It is the general path's behaviour, unreachable from the new builders (`scan` accepts only bulk strings). The new probe path handles it exactly like the old one. Recorded as a follow-up |

Checked and confirmed correct by the reviewer:
- **Builder equivalence** for names (ASCII and Unicode-only spellings), the UTF-8-before-arity order, `is_payload_index`, `validate_key`, parser limits and DEDUP envelopes.
- **Probe accounting** for WRONGTYPE, expired revival, no-op SADD, the list representation and the set's inline→table switch.
- **`growth_bound`** is a true upper bound, `creating_keys` lists all three commands, and reservations are released on every path.
- **Locking:** no window for a duplicated "before".
- **Untouched:** DEDUP unwrapping, ESet/EAdd, AOF, replication and tenant checks.
