# v0.6.0 Phase 6N-C4: why coalesced fan-out regressed at pipeline depth 16

## Summary

6N-C's Gate M1 found that `RECACHED_AOF_FANOUT=coalesced` was faster than `inline` at P=1 (1.58×) but slower at P=16 (0.69×, p99 8.9 ms against 4.9 ms). The batches it wrote held 1.2 frames, and its back-off never engaged.

There were **two** defects in `AofWriter::append_coalesced` (`server-native/src/persistence.rs`, as of `dev` `ef2c8c9`), not one:

1. **False-positive gain signal.** A yield counted as a gain when `done.is_complete() || Arc::strong_count(&done) > holders`. "Another writer performed my batch" is just as true of a batch that stayed at one frame, so nearly every yield counted as a gain, and the back-off (2 misses → lead at once for 32) never engaged.
2. **Premature lead after resuming.** A writer called `lead()` after its yield even when its own batch had already been written. At P=16 the queue then held the batch opened since, which contained one frame: the *next* pipelined command of the writer that had resumed just before. This writer performed that batch before any other writer could join it. Every resuming writer did the same, so batches stayed at about one frame.

The brief named the first defect. The second was found while reproducing it, and a native ablation shows that **it, not the gain signal, is what kept batches at 1.2 frames**. Fixing only the gain signal turns the P=16 regression into a P=1 regression (see [the ablation](#ablation-on-native-linux)).

## How a coalesced append ran (6N-C)

```text
queue_only(frame)                                        -- under the caller's write-order stripes
if should_yield():
    holders = strong_count(done)
    yield_now().await                                    -- woken after the worker's other ready tasks
    observe(gained = done.is_complete() || strong_count(done) > holders)
lead()                                                   -- perform every queued batch, in order
gate_wait(done).await
```

A tokio worker wakes a yielded task only after it has run every other ready task (and polled the I/O driver), or at its maintenance tick every 61 polls.

### P=1: 50 connections, each with one command ready

1. Connection A queues frame a1 into open batch B1 and yields. Connections B…Z, made ready by the same `epoll` pass, run and queue b1…z1 into B1, and each yields.
2. A resumes first, leads, and writes B1 with its N frames.
3. Each of the others resumes, finds B1 written, and calls `lead()`. The queue is usually empty, because a connection's next command has not arrived yet, so `lead()` does nothing.

Result: 4.7 frames per batch. The yield paid for itself.

### P=16: 50 connections, each with 16 commands buffered

1. Round 1 is the same as P=1, and A writes B1.
2. A, without a socket round trip, runs its *second* command: it queues a2 into a new batch B2 and yields.
3. B resumes. Its batch B1 is complete, which counts as a "gain". It calls `lead()`, which performs **B2 with only a2 in it**. B then queues b2 into B3 and yields.
4. C resumes, B1 complete → "gain", and its `lead()` writes B3 with only b2 in it. And so on.

Every command therefore paid a scheduler round trip and a `write(2)` of about one frame. Every yield was judged a gain, so the back-off never stopped the yields. The yields also interleaved the 50 pipelines, which delayed each pipeline's last reply: p99 went from 4.9 to 8.9 ms.

Pipeline depth is the whole difference. A resumed writer leads a batch it has no frame in only when someone has queued *after* its batch was written but *before* it resumed. At P=1 nobody has anything left to queue. At P=16 the writer that resumed just before has always queued its next command by then.

## Evidence

### Counters from Gate M1 (6N-C binary)

Raw data: `../6n-c/fedora-i5-9400f-20261119/results.txt`. 6N-C reported the fan-out path's yields in the shared `coalesce_*` counters.

| `coalesced` cell, per run | Yields judged gained | Yields judged missed | Frames per batch |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| P=1 | ≈ 1,000,190 | 1–2 | 4.7 |
| P=16 | ≈ 1,000,130 | 2–4 | 1.2 |

### Deterministic reproduction

`a_batch_another_writer_wrote_with_nothing_joined_is_a_miss` (in `server-native/src/tests/coalesced_fanout.rs`) reproduces defect 1 exactly. Writer A queues and yields. A `tail().settled()`, which adds no frame, writes A's batch. A then resumes. The 6N-C heuristic scores this as a gain. Mutation M1 (restoring that heuristic) makes the test fail. See [correctness tests](V0.6N-C4-CORRECTNESS-TESTS.md).

`skipping_the_lead_strands_nothing_when_the_next_writer_is_cancelled` reproduces the interleaving of defect 2: A's batch is written, C opens the next batch, and A resumes. It asserts that A does not write C's batch. Mutation M3 (always lead) fails it.

### Ablation on native Linux

On 2026-11-19, on the M1 host (Fedora, i5-9400F, governor `performance`), with the M1 workload unchanged:
- one primary worker, one replica, AOF `everysec`;
- 50 clients, `SET` of 32-byte values, 1,000,000 requests;
- two interleaved repetitions per cell, run with `6n-c/run-m1.sh` unmodified.

Three binaries were compared. Raw data: [`fedora-i5-9400f-20261119-ablation/`](fedora-i5-9400f-20261119-ablation/), driver `diag.sh`.

What this shows:

- **The gain signal was wrong, and correcting it is necessary but not sufficient.** With only the exact signal, P=16 backs off 31,750 times per run and drops to inline behaviour (186k against 178k inline). Its p99 gets *worse*, at 10.95 ms. P=1 also loses 14% throughput (76.9k against 89.9k). The premature leads still split batches, and the now-honest signal reports those splits as misses and backs off from yields that were in fact helping.
- **The premature lead is what kept batches small.** With both fixes, P=16 batches hold 27 frames (0.037 `write(2)` per command) and P=1 batches hold 14.4 (against 4.7 in 6N-C).
- **The two fixes are consistent with each other.** With both, about one yield per batch is a miss, from the last writer to join (missed ≈ batches: 36,843 against 37,027 at P=16). Back-off still engages a few times per million commands, where a yield really gains nothing.
- **CPU moved the right way.** Per op, CPU drops 8.14 → 7.18 µs at P=1 and 6.90 → 3.65 µs at P=16. P=16 is now 1.32 µs/op *below* `inline`.

## Not the cause

- **The connection gate (standalone, deferred appends) uses the same "complete or more holders" signal.** Its standalone M1 cell records about 1,001,500 "gained" against 5 "missed" per million commands. It was left unchanged, as the brief requires. Standalone throughput was validated in 6N-1 A and 6N-C. Whether the gate's signal also over-reports is a separate question for a later milestone; see the [release gate](V0.6N-C4-RELEASE-GATE.md).
- **Write-order stripe contention.** A writer that holds a stripe another writer needs makes that writer wait, so its yield gains nothing from it. With 256 stripes and 50 random-key writers, this costs about 4–5 collisions per round, which is minor. A key written by many pipelines in lock step serializes completely. Those yields are now correctly misses, and the back-off holds them to a few percent of appends (Test C, serialized variant).
- **Multiple workers.** Both the heuristic and the skipped lead are per batch. A second worker only adds writers that may join. See the 4-worker cells in [M1](V0.6N-C4-M1-PERFORMANCE.md).
