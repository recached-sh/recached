The history checker (Test D) treats the keyspace as one register: every MSET writes every key with a fresh generation. It checks that:
- every transaction sees one generation;
- no read is stale (a write that finished before it began is not missed);
- no read is inverted (new, then old).

It runs on 1 and 4 workers, ordered and unordered, with a MULTI-of-SETs writer among the writers. It has its own test of what it must refuse.

## 5. Redis differential

`server-native/tests/redis_diff.rs`. CI's redis-compat job runs it against Redis 7.2.5.
- `new_commands_match_redis`: unchanged, passes.
- `new_commands_match_redis_for_watch_and_transactions` (new): two connections per server and 27 cases. They cover WATCH before and after expiry, expiry swept, read or recreated, absent keys (stable, deleted, flushed, created, created past their deadline, created with PX 1, deleted alongside another key), live keys (deleted, flushed, TTL changed, persisted, SETNX no-op), empty STOREs, several keys, duplicate and missing keys, UNWATCH, DISCARD, repeated WATCH/UNWATCH, expiry between MULTI and EXEC, and read-only and mixed EXEC replies.
- **0 mismatches.**

Concurrent histories are not compared reply by reply. They are checked against a sequential specification (Tests A–C) or a register-linearizability model (Test D), with the legal interleavings stated in each test. The reviewer added 30 more probe cases across rounds; all matched Redis after the fixes.
