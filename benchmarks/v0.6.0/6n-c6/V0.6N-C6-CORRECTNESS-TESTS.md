## PR #144: queue and backpressure (`server-native/src/tests/repl_sender_batching.rs`)

These build on the 6N-C5 harness: the real `handle_replica` over TCP, against a scripted replica.

| Brief scenario | Test | Checks |
|---|---|---|
| **A**, the original reproduction | `pipelined_rounds_at_default_capacity_with_acks` (was `#[ignore]`, now a regression) | 40 writers × 128 frames × 8 rounds against an acknowledging replica, at the default limits. All 40,960 frames arrive once, contiguously. The replica stays connected. `queue_disconnects` is 0. The only synchronization is the initial partial one. The sender is never kept from its queue for more than a round (peak ≤ 10,240; the review measured 2,944–4,608). The charge is 0 at the end. On the old cap it fails in most runs. |
| A, its boundary | `pipelined_rounds_against_an_explicit_4096_frame_cap_lose_nothing` | `RECACHED_REPL_BUFFER=4096` is still strict (peak ≤ 4096). If the round overruns it (9 of 10 runs in review), the replica is dropped once and resumes from the backlog with exactly the rest. Both outcomes are checked. |
| **B**, just above the old limit | `one_poll_of_more_than_4096_frames_fits_the_default_queue` | One poll queues 6000 frames before the sender can run (deterministic). All are delivered and the replica stays connected. The peak is exactly 6000 frames, and the peak bytes is exactly Σ(len + 128). |
| B, at the limit | `a_burst_fills_the_default_queue_to_exactly_its_byte_budget` | Exactly ⌊8 MiB / 228⌋ frames of 100 B fit, and one more disconnects. The refused frame reserves nothing. |
| B, tiny frames | `tiny_frames_are_bounded_by_the_overhead_charge` | Exactly 65,536 zero-length frames fit, and the next is refused: tiny frames cannot queue without bound. |
| B, explicit cap | `an_explicit_frame_cap_is_strict_and_a_refused_frame_releases_its_charge` | A refusal at the frame cap releases its reservation, and draining releases all of it |
| B, repeated bursts | `repeated_bursts_twice_the_old_limit_keep_a_healthy_replica` | 4 bursts of 64 writers × 128 frames (8192 each) on 2 workers, with pauses to drain: kept, contiguous |
| B, pure functions | `the_queue_charge_and_frame_cap_are_bounded` | The charge saturates, an explicit cap is exact, the derived cap is clamped to [1, 2²⁰], and `RECACHED_REPL_BUFFER` parsing handles unset, 0, negative, unparsable, padded and huge values |
| **C**, small channels | `small_explicit_channels_keep_rounds_that_fit_and_resume_after_one_that_does_not` | For 16, 64 and 256 frames: rounds that fit never disconnect, wherever the sender lands. A burst of cap + 1 before the sender can run disconnects, the connection closes after at most a contiguous prefix, and the resume serves exactly the rest. |
| C, 6N-C5 | `a_small_channel_is_drained_on_every_wake` | Unchanged and passing |
| **D**, large frames | `frames_near_the_byte_budget_are_charged_exactly` | budget − 128 fits exactly; budget − 127 is refused without reserving anything |
| D | `large_frames_never_queue_past_the_byte_budget`, and the 6N-C5 test `large_frames_do_not_overflow_a_batching_sender` | 256 KiB frames against a 1 MiB budget from 16 writers: the sender never finds more than the budget, and any disconnect resumes exactly |
| **E**, slow replica | `a_stalled_replica_at_the_default_limits_is_dropped_within_its_budget` | A replica that stops reading. No queue is ever charged past 8 MiB (checked every 64 writes). Writers never stall. It is dropped once, after more than 4096 frames (the budget decided, not a frame count). It resumes with exactly the missing range, and the live stream continues. INFO shows 1 disconnect and 2 partial syncs. |
| E, review M1 | `an_unregistered_replica_that_keeps_its_socket_open_is_closed` | Once dropped, its sender ends within 10 s **while the peer still holds its socket open**. Nothing stays charged, and the peer finds the stream closed. |
| E, 6N-C5 | `a_stalled_replica_is_dropped_and_resumes_from_the_backlog` | Unchanged and passing |
| **F**, several replicas | `a_stalled_replica_is_dropped_while_a_healthy_one_keeps_every_frame` | Two replicas: the stalled one is dropped on its own budget. The healthy one keeps every frame, contiguously, through 1000 more writes. Neither queue is ever charged past its budget. |
| **G**, cancellation | `aborting_the_sender_with_frames_still_queued_releases_their_charge` | The sender is aborted before it ever drains. Every charge is released, nothing reaches the replica, the next write unregisters it, and the resume serves each frame exactly once. |
| G, 6N-C5 | `aborting_the_sender_before_its_round_flush_strands_nothing` | Unchanged and passing |
| **H**, recovery | `a_dropped_replica_past_the_backlog_takes_a_full_synchronization` | A replica dropped for its budget whose offset the backlog no longer holds is refused a partial sync, takes a full one (mode `F`), and the live stream continues from the snapshot's offset. INFO shows (1 partial, 1 full). |
| H | Partial resume after a drop: scenarios C, E and the explicit-4096 test above. DEDUP replay and promotion: `replication_dedup::*`, `integration_6nc::a_promoted_replica_stops_applying_its_old_primarys_stream`, `dedup_order_tests`. Full resync under concurrent writes: `repl_6l_cpu::replicas_reattach_partially_and_fully_while_writes_continue`. Replica restart: `replication_dedup::a_full_synchronization_carries_the_dedup_table`. | All pass unchanged in both modes |
| **I**, security | `unauthenticated_and_invalid_handshakes_never_reach_a_replica_queue` | With a replication password: no queue exists for a peer held mid-authentication while 100 frames are fanned out, nor for a wrong password (`-ERR`), nor for an authenticated peer with an invalid resume request. No synchronization is counted. |
| I | `tests.rs::repeated_bad_replication_passwords_block_the_peer`; `multitenant::*`, `ws_auth::*`, `security_v06_regressions::*` | All pass. Tenant isolation does not touch the replication queue. Aggregate memory is N replicas × the budget, with N bounded by the listener's connection permits. |
| INFO | `info_replication_reports_both_redis_and_recached_spellings` | The four new fields |
