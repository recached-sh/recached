## The twelve questions

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Was the healthy-replica overflow reproduced? | **Yes.** On 10 of 10 runs at exactly 10,112 frames, identically with acknowledgements off. On the real server, at 100 × P=64: 3–5 disconnects per run. |
| 2 | Root cause? | A maintenance park (every 61 polls) mid-round fires the sender's held-flush deferral, or an acknowledgement's readiness, and queues the sender behind the round's remaining writers. Their `try_send` wakes cannot move a notified task, so the channel must absorb up to a whole round, and one connection's poll can exceed 4096 small frames on its own. The 4096-frame cap filled with 27.6 KB queued, **0.33% of the byte budget**. |
| 3 | Which bounded solution? | **Option B:** size the queue by bytes. Each frame charges its payload + 128 B; the frame cap is derived from the budget unless set explicitly. Review M1 added closing an unregistered replica's connection. |
| 4 | Does the original reproduction pass? | **Yes.** All 40,960 frames arrive, with no disconnect and no resync. |
| 5 | Is memory bounded under all tested conditions? | **Yes.** At most 8 MiB charged per replica, and the item count is bounded independently. Tested at the exact budget, at budget + 1, with zero-length frames, near-budget frames, a saturating charge, refusals, aborts, and stalled replicas with and without an open socket. |
| 6 | Do slow replicas still trigger backpressure? | **Yes.** They are dropped at the byte budget without the primary waiting; on the real server, 2 s stopped means one drop per run, and the connection is now closed at once. |
| 7 | Partial and full resync correct? | **Yes.** An exact partial resume after every kind of drop, a full resync once the backlog has moved on, and the live stream continuing from the boundary (tests). Real server: every run converged with matching samples. |
| 8 | ACK cancellation fixed? | **Yes**, in its own PR (#145), without a protocol change. Only the lag metric ever read the value. |
| 9 | Configuration defaults changed? | **Yes, as approved:** the derived frame cap (65,536, was 4096) and the 128 B per-frame charge. An explicit `RECACHED_REPL_BUFFER` is still strict, so `RECACHED_REPL_BUFFER=4096` restores the old cap. The `recached_replication_queue_bytes` gauge now includes the charge. Documented in configuration, operations, protocol, upgrading-0.6 and the CHANGELOG. |
| 10 | P=1 and P=16 acceptable? | **Yes.** Within ±3% throughput and ±1.5% CPU per op against 6N-C5's byte-identical binary, interleaved in both orders. |
| 11 | Security and durability preserved? | **Yes.** No queue before authentication; budgets per replica; AOF ordering and fail-stop unchanged. |
| 12 | Remaining release blockers? | Below |

## Conditions

1. **Merge** #146, #144 and #145 (CI green) and PR 4 once its CI is green. #144 and #145 merge in either order with the same result.
2. **Include #144 in the final release-candidate soak.** It changes what happens under overload: a replica is dropped by bytes rather than after 4096 frames, and a dropped replica's connection is closed at once. Per the brief, no separate soak was run in this phase.
3. **Performance was measured on a noisy host** (6.9 GB swapped, 4–14% IO pressure). The conclusions rest on CPU per operation, cells without AOF, and order-reversed repetitions. A quiet-host rerun of `rc-coalesced-P64` would settle that cell's −2% to −6% spread, which sits inside fsync noise (follow-up).
